These came straight out of a run on a real app. Before shotfleet, its iOS listing had the same English screenshots copied into every language folder.

Found on the first run

Users in four languages couldn't finish onboarding.

On a common small phone, the Training Goals page pushed its Next button off the bottom in Japanese, Korean, Chinese and Arabic, and the page didn't scroll. English fit, so nobody noticed. shotfleet's Chinese run got stuck there, which led to a two-line fix.

Screenshot runs are the only time most teams walk through the app in every language. shotfleet makes that walk useful.

How it works

Your existing flow, no app changes.

  1. Write the flow once, in English

    A normal Maestro flow: tapOn: "Next", takeScreenshot: 01_home. Or point shotfleet at the flow you already have.

  2. shotfleet translates it

    It reads the strings compiled into your .app or APK, so "Next" becomes (Nächste|Weiter) in German, 次へ in Japanese. No accessibility IDs, no per-language copies.

  3. Every language runs at once

    Simulators and emulators at store sizes (1320×2868 for iPhone; 1080×1920, the tallest phone shape Google Play accepts), each set to one language, with a 9:41 status bar.

  4. Checked, then exported

    Each image is read back and compared to your app's strings. Passing languages go straight into fastlane's deliver and supply folders.

What it checks

Catches what a quick look misses.

  • Wrong languageThe German folder shows English or Japanese strings.
  • CopiesThe same image in two languages.
  • Missing imagesA language that stopped part-way through the flow.
  • Word clashesBefore any run: Turkish "Next" and "Advanced" are both İleri, so a tap could hit the wrong button.
