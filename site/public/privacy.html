Privacy

What I keep, and how to delete it.

By default this site sets no cookies and loads nothing from other servers. Google Analytics runs only if you press Accept analytics, and below I explain what that involves. Whatever you choose, on the home page the site adds one to a running total for each page view and for a few actions (reaching the run or pricing section, clicking a button), split by day, by the site or link you came from, and by your browser's language. Those totals are not linked to you and your IP address is not stored. Beyond that, the only things stored are your analytics choice, in your own browser, and what you sent if you joined the early-access list before the sale opened, or send when you write to me.

Analytics (only if you accept)

The first time you visit, a banner asks whether I may use Google Analytics 4 to see how this site is used. Accept analytics and Decline are equal choices. Until you accept, nothing is loaded from Google and no cookie is set. If you decline, or your browser blocks local storage, that stays true. I keep your choice in your browser's local storage under the name shotfleet-analytics, not in a cookie.

If you accept:

  • Your browser loads a script from googletagmanager.com and sends data to Google Analytics: the page address and title, the site that referred you, your browser, operating system, screen size and language, your approximate location, the time, and a few actions I chose (clicking a button, reaching the run or pricing section). It does not send your email address.
  • Two cookies are set on shotfleet.com, _ga and _ga_ followed by an id from my property. They hold a random identifier so repeat visits can be told apart, and they last up to 13 months.
  • On your IP address, Google documents that Google Analytics 4 does not log or store IP addresses. I never see it.
  • Google signals and the advertising features are off, and the consent settings keep ad storage, ad user data and ad personalization denied. Only analytics storage is granted.
  • Google keeps the event data for up to 14 months and then deletes it.

The legal basis is your consent. Google acts as my processor and may process the data in the United States and other countries under its own terms; see Google's privacy policy. To withdraw, use Analytics settings in the footer of any page and choose Decline: it stops the data and deletes the two cookies. Clearing this site's data in your browser does the same. You can also change your mind there and accept later.

This is separate from the first-party counter described at the top of the page, which keeps page-view totals by source and language, sets no cookie, stores no IP address, and counts every visit because it records nothing about you.

When you buy

Purchases go through Gumroad (privacy policy), which sells shotfleet to you as the merchant of record. Gumroad passes me the details of your order, which can include your name, email and country (its privacy policy says exactly what), so I can support you. The installed program checks your licence key with Gumroad when you activate it and about once a week after. It sends Gumroad your key, the product ID, and whether the call should count as an activation. Activating makes two calls: the first only looks at the key, and the second counts it, and that second call is made only when this Mac doesn't have the key saved yet. The weekly check never counts. It sends no Mac name, no activation id and no program version, and the request carries your IP address and Python's standard user-agent string. It sends Gumroad nothing else. shotfleet doctor also asks this site for the latest version number; that is an ordinary page request and I don't record it. If you let shotfleet doctor --fix download what's missing, it gets Maestro from GitHub, and asks Adoptium for Java and downloads it from the link Adoptium gives. For Android it gets Google's command-line tools (through Homebrew if you have it, otherwise from Google), and then Google's own tool downloads the rest from Google, with its usage reporting turned off where the tool allows it. The downloads shotfleet makes itself carry your IP address and shotfleet's version number (in the user-agent string), and nothing about your app. Your app and screenshots are never uploaded.

If you joined the list before the sale opened

Until the sale opened, the site had an early-access list. It has no signup form now. If you joined it, this is what I stored:

  • Your email address.
  • What you build with, if you answered the optional question.
  • Your country, which Cloudflare works out from your IP address, and your browser's language. The IP address itself isn't stored.
  • The site that linked you here and any utm_ tags in the link, so I know which post people came from.
  • The date and time you joined.

What I use it for

To send you one email when shotfleet is available to buy (the sale is open now, and that one email is about the launch), and to count how many people are interested. I don't share it, sell it, or add you to any other list. When someone joined, my server sent me an email through Cloudflare with the signup number, the country, the source and the stack answer. It never contains the email address.

Where it's kept, and for how long

In Cloudflare Workers KV, the storage for the small server behind this page. Cloudflare also processes your IP address to serve the page and to limit how often the forms can be sent. I will delete the waitlist within 30 days after I send the launch email, or sooner if you ask. Messages you send through the contact form are kept until I've answered them, with the reply address if you gave one and your country (worked out from your IP address, which isn't stored), and I delete them on request.

Remove your email

Enter the address you signed up with and it's deleted right away (Cloudflare can take up to a minute to clear its copies everywhere), together with any contact messages you sent with that address as the reply address.

Done. If that address was on the list, it's gone.
