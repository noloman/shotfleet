# Dutch Tax & Registration for Software Sales via Gumroad

**Disclaimer:** This is research from primary sources, not legal or tax advice. Consult a Dutch accountant or the Belastingdienst for your specific situation.

## 1. KvK Registration Requirement

**Must you register?** Yes—you must register with KvK from the moment you become economically active (selling your first product or launching a website). [KvK.nl](https://www.kvk.nl/starten/op-welke-datum-moet-ik-mijn-bedrijf-inschrijven-bij-kvk/)

**Business vs. Hobby distinction:** The Belastingdienst distinguishes based on these eight criteria:
- Ongoing profitability (not consistent losses)
- Independence in operations
- Capital invested in tools/marketing
- Adequate time commitment
- Multiple clients
- Marketing effort (website, social media)
- Entrepreneurial risk (payment defaults, market demand)
- Personal liability for debts

[Belastingdienst: Entrepreneur Status](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/winst/inkomstenbelasting/wanneer_bent_u_ondernemer_voor_de_inkomstenbelasting/)

Occasional sales or hobby sales (e.g., selling surplus items at a loss) are not taxable. Systematic sales of a product for profit—even below €20,000/year—constitute a business. [Belastingdienst: Online Sales](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/werk-en-inkomen/content/inkomstenbelasting-betalen-over-internetverkopen)

**Action:** Shotfleet (€29–€49 per licence, ongoing sales) is clearly a business. KvK registration is required, typically within one week of first sale.

## 2. VAT (btw) when Gumroad is merchant of record (corrected on review, partly inference)

Verified on the sources the research cites: Gumroad says it is the merchant of record, charges and remits buyers' VAT and sales tax itself, and the Belastingdienst's KOR (kleineondernemersregeling) is an optional exemption for entrepreneurs with turnover up to EUR 20,000 a year.

Not verified, and an earlier draft got it wrong: that "above EUR 20,000 you must register for btw". The EUR 20,000 line only decides whether you may use the KOR. A Dutch entrepreneur normally gets a btw number when registering with the KvK and files returns unless the KOR applies. Whether your supplies to Gumroad (a US platform) are outside Dutch btw, or reverse-charged, is a question for an accountant. Do not rely on this file for it.

Open questions for the accountant: (1) is the supply to Gumroad outside the scope of Dutch btw, and do you still need to file returns; (2) should you use the KOR; (3) what to enter as income (Gumroad's payout statements show gross sales, fees and taxes).

## 3. Gumroad's position on international sellers

Gumroad states it is the merchant of record for all sales and remits VAT. It asks non-US sellers for a tax form (W-8BEN) in its payout settings. The withholding rate that applies to a Dutch seller under the Netherlands-US treaty is NOT verified here: an earlier draft stated 30% falling to 15%, which is unchecked. Ask the accountant or read Gumroad's tax-form page before filling the form.

## 4. Practical Next Steps

**Checklist for Shotfleet owner:**

1. **Register with KvK immediately** as eenmanszaak at [kvk.nl](https://www.kvk.nl/starten/eenmanszaak-zo-vul-je-het-online-inschrijfformulier-in/). KvK notifies the Belastingdienst automatically.

2. **Confirm Gumroad's VAT handling** in its sales reports (the checkout already adds Dutch VAT: a Dutch buyer pays $29 plus $6.09 VAT, seen in the test purchase). Whether you need btw returns is an accountant question (section 2).

3. **Ask about the KOR** (optional exemption up to EUR 20,000 turnover) when you register.

4. **W-8BEN:** complete it if Gumroad asks; check the treaty rate with the accountant first.

5. **Keep records:** Retain sales invoices, Gumroad statements, and payout records for the Belastingdienst.

6. **Consult an accountant or Belastingdienst directly** for inkomstenbelasting (income tax) filing and eligible deductions (zelfstandigenaftrek). Your first tax return is due by 1 May of the following year.
